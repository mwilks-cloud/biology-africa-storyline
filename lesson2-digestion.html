Breaking down prey protein into amino acids to build lion tissue!

💡 Guiding Question

When a lion eats zebra muscle (protein), it can't directly attach zebra muscle onto its own body. Why must the lion break prey protein down into single amino acids before rebuilding them?

Step 1: Chemical Digestion vs. Biosynthesis

✂️

1. Digestion (Hydrolysis)

Stomach acid and enzymes break bonds in food protein, separating long chains into free amino acids.

🏗️

2. Protein Synthesis (Biosynthesis)

Lion ribosomes recombine those free amino acids into specific sequences to build lion muscle, hair, and enzymes.

Step 2: The Path of an Amino Acid

Here is what happens step-by-step when an organism eats protein:

  1. Ingestion: Food polymers (proteins, starches, fats) enter the digestive system.
  2. Breakdown: Stomach acid and digestive enzymes sever chemical bonds, releasing individual monomers (amino acids, glucose).
  3. Absorption & Transport: Monomers pass through the intestinal wall into the bloodstream and are delivered to cells.
  4. Rebuilding: Ribosomes inside cells bond amino acids back together in a specific order to build new muscle tissue, enzymes, and hair!

1. What single building block (monomer) must a lion absorb into its blood to build muscle protein?

🔍 Click here to reveal answer & explanation
✅ Answer: Amino Acids

Explanation: Proteins are polymers built out of amino acid monomers. Digestion breaks down food proteins into single amino acids so cells can recombine them into lion muscle tissue.

2. Why do different proteins (like zebra muscle vs. lion muscle) have different structures and functions?

🔍 Click here to reveal answer & explanation
✅ Answer: The order and arrangement of amino acids

Explanation: Just as changing the order of letters creates different words, changing the specific sequence of amino acids builds entirely different functional proteins!
